# Abstract

HVAC service providers such as Aire Serv make three kinds of decisions every week: how many technicians and how much stock a hot or cold spell will need, which customer machines to inspect before they fail, and which customers to phone before they stop buying. We built HVAC Service Planner, a small dashboard that turns three machine learning predictions into short lists a manager can act on: an hourly power demand curve, a ranked inspection list and a ranked call list.

We had no data from a real HVAC company, so each prediction is trained on a public stand-in dataset: a year of hourly HVAC power readings (8,760 rows), the AI4I 2020 machine failure data (10,000 machines, 3.4% failures) and the Online Retail II purchase history (5,939 customers). We compare two models of our choice, Ridge regression and XGBoost, with ordinary linear or logistic regression, a Random Forest and a rule of thumb that a company could use today. Every score comes from data the model did not see: random hours, unseen months, a forecast of the last three months, a heatwave at or above 40 °C, unseen machines and unseen customers in a later period. Each experiment is repeated with five random seeds.

The main results are as follows. For demand, XGBoost misses by 0.065 kW on average, against 0.93 kW for the rule of thumb, but in the heatwave test its error grows to 0.34 kW, because tree models cannot predict beyond the temperatures they were trained on. Ridge regression extends the trend and misses by 0.08 kW there, so the dashboard uses Ridge for hours above 40 °C. For failure, XGBoost and Random Forest find 94% of failures in the 10% of machines they rank highest, against 36% for inspecting the most worn tools first. For churn, every model reaches about 0.78 ROC-AUC and finds 20% of lapsing customers in the top 10% of the list, twice as many as random contact and four points more than the recency rule. That gain is real but modest.

We recommend piloting the Demand Planner with the heatwave safeguard and the Failure Radar on real company data, and treating the Retention List as a low-cost aid until richer customer data are available. The report follows the ten stages of the machine learning lifecycle, from problem definition to monitoring, and each stage was written by one member of the group.

Keywords: HVAC, demand forecasting, predictive maintenance, customer churn, Ridge regression, XGBoost, held-out evaluation, model monitoring.
